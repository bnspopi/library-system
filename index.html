Two faces of the same database

Staff and the public are looking at one set of records through two very different windows. The availability a reader sees in the catalogue is the same item.status the circulation desk wrote a second ago. There is no export, no overnight sync, no second entry.

⌗

Staff side

Catalogers, the circulation desk, acquisitions, serials and administration, each with their own module, all writing to one shared file.

  • Issue, return and renew against a live policy matrix
  • Create bibliographic records and attach copies
  • Order, encumber, receive, invoice and accession
  • Predict, check in, claim and bind serial issues
  • Configure the rules that drive every due date

Public side

The OPAC or discovery layer, the mobile account, and the self-check kiosk, all speaking to exactly the same circulation engine.

  • Search every branch, format and licensed e-resource at once
  • Real-time availability, because it is the same record

  • Renew online, refused for the same reasons the desk would refuse

How the pieces sit together

Three classic components: a database, software that talks to it, and two interfaces. What makes it integrated is the single layer of business rules sitting between them, so nothing can reach the data and bypass policy.

Business rules & policy The brain

Eligibility, due-date calculation, hold capture, renewal caps, the fine formula, budget encumbrance. This layer is transactional: a checkout either fully succeeds or rolls back, so a crashed scanner can never leave a copy both “out” and “in”.

Shared relational database One source of truth

Bibs, items, patrons and transactions in one schema, indexed on ISBN, barcode, title keywords and call number, with audit and history tables so any record can be reconstructed.

Integrations Outbound

Payment gateway, email and SMS, RFID and security gates, union catalogues, the e-resource knowledge base, and an off-site replica, because losing the circulation database on a Monday morning is a disaster.

Deployment shapes differ, the layers do not. A classic ILS such as Koha, Symphony or Polaris runs this as a monolith against one database, often on-premises. A modern Library Services Platform (Alma, FOLIO, WorldShare) runs the same layers as cloud services behind an API gateway, and treats print and electronic resources in one workflow.

Seven people, one system

Each role touches a different module, but every one of them is reading and writing the same records. That is the whole point, and the reason permissions are role-based rather than all-or-nothing.

Why two people get different due dates

Checkout is never “14 days for everyone”. The engine resolves member category × item type × location, then adjusts for the calendar, existing blocks and the hold queue. Change the controls below: this is the live engine from the demo, not a mock-up.

Closed days are read from the library calendar. When they are skipped, a due date that would land on a closing day rolls forward to the next open one.
